Surgical Robotics Research

Two connected lines of work in robotic surgery simulation. The first is surgical digital twin modelling, in which finite element models of soft tissue and embedded tumours are built to stay physically continuous under gravity and instrument contact while running at interactive rates, and are palpated to recover contact forces for subsurface tumour localization. The second is semi-automated motion primitives, an execution layer that turns sparse human waypoints into repeatable, randomizable trajectories for both rigid instruments and deformable tissue, so that physically consistent interaction data can be generated at scale for surgical autonomy.

Maintained by Abhinaba Bhattacharjee, PhD candidate in Robotics and Biomechanics.

Liver Tumor Surgical Twin

Three finite element paradigms for representing a stiff tumour inside softer liver tissue, compared under gravity and under robotic palpation, with the goal of a digital twin that stays continuous and still runs at interactive frame rates.

IROS 2026 Workshop  ·  Project page
Semi-Automated Motion Primitives

A supervisable execution layer for a simulated da Vinci Research Kit, in which one Grasp-Lock formulation covers both rigid and deformable objects without a change of code path, evaluated across six Fundamentals of Robotic Surgery inspired tasks.

In preparation
SimVinci

The full real-time surgical simulation framework that the projects above are built on. A five-layer architecture spanning task specification, human supervision, motion primitives, trajectory generation and constraint-based multiphysics, compatible with the da Vinci Research Kit.
