Semi-Automated Motion Primitives for Rigid and
Deformable Manipulation in Simulated Robotic Surgery

1Mechanical Engineering  ·  2Electrical and Computer Engineering  ·  5Computer Science, Purdue University

Abstract

Learning-based surgical autonomy is limited less by model capacity than by the availability of physically consistent interaction data. Producing that data in simulation requires an execution layer that is repeatable, supervisable, and equally valid for rigid instruments and deformable tissue.

We present a semi-automated motion primitive framework for a simulated da Vinci Research Kit patient side manipulator, built on the SOFA multiphysics engine. Four basic primitives compose into four derived primitives beneath a supervision layer in which a human specifies sparse waypoints and the primitives generate the executed trajectory. Grasping is handled by a single Grasp-Lock formulation that covers rigid and deformable objects without a change of code path.

Across 25 randomized episodes per task on six Fundamentals of Robotic Surgery inspired tasks, the framework sustains 40 to 65 frames per second, succeeds in every isolated episode, and reaches 84 to 100 percent success on the cascaded tasks.

Layered architecture

The framework is organized in five layers. Command and intent descend from the task and supervision layers through recomposable primitives to the physics engine, while state and jaw constraint forces propagate back up.

Task
A finite state machine that sequences a task into primitive calls.
Supervision
Accepts sparse human waypoints, entered through a control interface, a game controller or a haptic device.
Primitive
Reach, Path Follow, Jaw open and close, and Homing form the basic set. Approach, Grasp, Transport and Release are derived from them, each with its own entry condition, termination condition and failure signal.
Trajectory
Converts primitive targets into joint-space Hermite splines and Cartesian SLERP interpolation.
Physics
Constraint-based rigid body and finite element simulation of the patient side manipulator with remote centre of motion kinematics, endo-wrist articulation and jaw collision models, coupled to rigid and deformable objects in SOFA.

The Reach primitive is reused inside every other primitive, so the whole set inherits one inverse kinematics and one termination implementation. That is what allows the primitives to be recomposed into long-horizon tasks without writing task-specific control code.

Grasp-Lock

Jaw closure is admittance controlled, so the commanded closure velocity is modulated by the resistance encountered at the jaws rather than driven to a fixed angle. When closure resistance indicates contact, Grasp-Lock engages.

For a rigid object, the object pose is expressed as a fixed homogeneous transform relative to the tool grasp frame, and the object is carried by that offset until release. For a deformable object, the finite element nodes lying within a capture region attached to the jaw frame are identified and bound to that frame, then released when the jaws open. Both cases are expressed through the same primitive interface, so Transport is invoked identically for a rigid needle, a rigid peg block and a compliant soft ring.

The capture region is not a tuning parameter. Its radius is taken directly from the instrument model as the distance from the gripper midpoint to the jaw tip, so it adapts across instruments without adjustment. The Large Needle Driver gives a capture radius of 3.0 mm and the Cadiere Forceps 8.0 mm.

Contact forces are recovered from Lagrange multipliers at constraints enforcing non-penetration, rather than from a penalty response to interpenetration, and no simulated force sensor is instrumented at the jaws. These signals are used to detect grasp onset, slip and collision events and to compare loading across repetitions. Because the interactive frame rates are obtained with a conjugate gradient solver paired to an uncoupled constraint correction, whose diagonal compliance approximation does not propagate the finite element stiffness into the multiplier, absolute magnitudes should be read as relative to the configuration rather than as calibrated forces.

Results

Six tasks derived from the Fundamentals of Robotic Surgery curriculum, in two families. The isolated tasks randomize object pose and initial jaw state and exercise the Grasp primitive alone. The cascaded tasks chain Approach, Grasp, Transport and Release with no intermediate human input, and therefore test whether primitive termination conditions compose without drift. Every task is run for 25 randomized episodes. Rows shaded gold are the deformable tasks.

A trial counts as successful when the object is released within 1.5 mm of the goal pose for rigid bodies and within 5 mm for deformable bodies, with no drop at any point in the episode. The cost of deformability shows up in release error and frame rate rather than in success rate: error rises from below 0.11 mm on the rigid objects to 2.76 mm on the soft ring, and frame rate falls from about 65 to 40.9 frames per second. Under composition, the dominant failure mode is orientation convergence at the seating waypoint rather than loss of the grasp itself.
